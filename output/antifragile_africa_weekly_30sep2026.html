Component 01
Executive Brief

Sovereign-Grade Weekly Assessment

KENYA'S REGULATOR PUBLISHED A LIST OF THE UNLICENSED. THE LIST OF THE LICENSED, AS FAR AS ACIOS CAN CONFIRM, IS STILL EMPTY, AND THE DEADLINE IS 35 DAYS OUT.

Kenya's Capital Markets Authority issued a public caution, dated 11 September 2026 and posted 15 September, naming 15 entities it says operate without authorisation, and stating that investigations have been referred to law enforcement. It predates Vol. 24 (16 September) by five days and was not in that issue. That is a research-scope failure, not a factual error in what was published, and it is disclosed here. The notice is the first enforcement-flavoured public act ACIOS has located from Kenya's regulators under the July regulations, and it is directionally consistent with the consumer-harm scenario tracked since Vol. 21.

Enforcement against the unlicensed has arrived before licensing of anyone. No source located this cycle confirms that CBK or CMA has granted any VASP licence since the Regulations took effect on 22 July, and none announces a grace period. The statutory transition date (4 November, Section 47 of the VASP Act) is 35 days away. [1st order] A regulator is naming violators. [2nd order] Naming violators without naming licensees tells the market where the perimeter is, but not who is inside it. [3rd order] If 4 November arrives with an empty registry, the CMA's public list becomes the only official map of the market, and a list of who is barred with no list of who is allowed will push users toward whichever operators publicly claim compliance, whether or not they hold a licence.

Nigeria's SEC remains silent 27 days after its 3 September comment close, and the Harmonised Implementation Framework is roughly 45 days overdue. No finalised rule, extension or Framework was located. P-21-01 (Framework published before 30 September) reaches its falsification date today and, on present evidence, resolves false. ACIOS also notes, from this cycle's research, a separate enacted track: an SEC capital circular of 16 January 2026 that sets a 30 June 2027 compliance deadline for existing capital market operators. That is distinct from the August draft tiers and had not been distinguished in earlier volumes; treat it as single-check until read against the primary circular.

South Africa's SARB/Treasury cross-border Crypto Asset Manual comment window closes today, 30 September. ACIOS has no newer information than Vol. 24 on this or on the SCA appeal, and reports both as carried forward rather than re-verified. Flutterwave's East African bank acquisition target remains unnamed in everything located this cycle.

Scope statement. This cycle's research went deep on Kenya and Nigeria and shallow elsewhere. Ghana, Zimbabwe, Morocco, Rwanda, WAEMU, Uganda, Zambia, Ethiopia, Yellow Card and MTN MoMo were not re-searched and are marked carried forward. The issue is also one week behind the 23 September target.

Key risk: The CMA notice was read through secondary reporting of its contents; ACIOS has not confirmed the entity list against the primary PDF and would revise if particulars differ. Absence of a located licence is absence of evidence: a grant could have occurred unreported.
Kenya's CMA names 15 unlicensed entities (11 Sep) with no licensee confirmed and 35 days to the 4 Nov deadline; Nigeria's SEC silence hits 27 days and P-21-01 falsifies today; South Africa's SARB window closes today; other markets carried forward.
Component 02
Top Non-Obvious Insights

What the Verified Record Actually Shows

HIGH
Naming the unlicensed before licensing anyone is a signalling choice with a hidden cost
A blacklist without a whitelist tells users what to avoid, not what to trust. Third order: that vacuum is filled by operators asserting compliance, and the first licence grant will carry outsized trust value for whoever holds it.
Sources: CMA public caution (11 Sep), press coverage [Tier 1/2]
MEDIUM
Enforcement posture may be set before the deadline, not on it
A regulator already naming violators 54 days before the transition date has a narrower choice on 4 November than Vol. 24 framed. Third order: a strict path now has a public precedent; a blanket grace period would have to reverse a posture already on the record.
Source: ACIOS analysis of CMA notice timing
MEDIUM
Nigeria has at least two capital-adequacy tracks on different clocks
The 16 January 2026 circular carries a 30 June 2027 deadline for existing operators; the 20 August draft proposes tiered floors. Third order: firms modelling off the draft alone may be planning against the wrong date.
Source: search results on SEC circular; single-check, primary text not read [Tier 2]
LOW-MEDIUM
A promised federal white paper on virtual assets may be a third unpublished Nigerian document
One legal-advisory source referred to a forthcoming white paper alongside the Council Framework. ACIOS could not confirm its status and lists it as unresolved.
Source: single legal-advisory reference [Tier 2/3]
